# Scenarios: when a player gets hurt

The owner asked (11 October): a coach's player breaks a hand. How do real academies handle it, and how should GrassKickZ? The research below comes from public guidance: the FA, NHS, St John Ambulance, NSPCC, UEFA, IFAB, children's hospitals and Georgian law. Sources are at the end. **Inference** marks our own reading, not a rule. Nothing here is medical advice: the treating clinician decides.

## 1. A broken hand, as real academies handle it

**At the pitch (first minutes)**
- **Who does what:**
  - The first-aid-trained adult treats the child. FA-accredited youth teams need a first-aid-trained coach, and the certificate lasts three years.
  - A second adult stays whenever a child is treated apart from the group.
  - The coach keeps the rest of the group supervised.
- **First aid:**
  - Support the arm above and below the injury, pad it, and put it in a sling.
  - Take off rings and watches.
  - Wrapped ice for up to 20 minutes.
  - Nothing to eat or drink, because surgery is possible.
- **Where to send the child:**
  - Call an ambulance (112 in Georgia) for an open fracture or a misshapen wrist. Also for a numb or tingling hand, heavy bleeding, or a suspected neck or back injury.
  - Otherwise go to the emergency department, with someone the child knows.
- **Parents** are called at once. The club records who told them and when.
- **The emergency plan** names the nearest hospital, emergency access, the defibrillator and the first-aid kit. It is reviewed every season and should be ready for every session and fixture.

**The same day**
- **An accident report**, following the NSPCC form:
  - the adult in charge and the child;
  - date, time and place;
  - what happened and how, and the witnesses;
  - the first aid given;
  - when and by whom the parents were told;
  - where the child went, whether they returned to play, and signatures.
- **Review:** a designated person keeps it and a senior person signs it off.
- **Insurance:** in England, personal-accident insurance has been compulsory for affiliated youth teams since 2017/18. In Georgia this is unknown (open question).
- **Welfare:** a safeguarding referral happens only if the cause raises a concern. It goes to the welfare officer; never promise the child secrecy.

**Diagnosis and healing.** An X-ray first, then a splint or cast; displaced bones are set, and bad breaks need surgery. Children heal fast, faster the younger they are, until the growth plates close (about 14 for girls, 16 for boys).

| Injury | Immobilised | Contact sport again |
|---|---|---|
| Buckle (torus) wrist fracture | Splint about 3 weeks; a soft bandage works as well as a cast (Lancet 2022, 965 children) | About 6 weeks; full sport at 4–6 weeks |
| Other forearm fractures | Cast 4–8 weeks | 4–6 weeks after the cast comes off; 2–3 months after surgery |
| Growth-plate (Salter-Harris) | About 6 weeks | Types I–II: 2–3 months. Types III–IV: X-rayed again at 6–12 months (about 4% risk of growth arrest) |
| Finger or hand | Buddy strapping 3 weeks | 4–6 weeks |
| Scaphoid (may not show on the first X-ray) | Cast 6–12 weeks | After clearance |

- **Clearance:** the treating doctor sets the return date, and clubs may ask for a letter.
- **Laws of the Game (Law 4):** no dangerous equipment. Soft, padded protection may be allowed, but the referee inspects it and decides. Hard casts are usually refused (Canada Soccer bans them even when padded).
- **Goalkeepers** have about five times the outfield rate of hand and arm injuries. **Inference:** no goalkeeping until cleared.

**Back to football (inference from the sources)**
1. **In the cast:** attends, and does leg and fitness work with no fall risk.
2. **Out of the cast:** non-contact drills.
3. **Cleared by the clinician:** full contact training.
4. **Match:** the referee checks any protection.

- FIFA counts a player as returned at full, unrestricted team training.
- Keep the child in the group: injured players often feel less a part of the team, and support from the team and coach helps recovery.

**Privacy.** Health data is special-category data. Georgia's data protection law, in force since 1 March 2024, needs a parent's written consent by default for a minor's health data.
- **Need to know:** the coach learns what the child can and cannot do ("no contact, no goalkeeping until 14 November"), not the diagnosis.
- **The child's voice:** the child is heard, and may choose an adult to be present during care.
- **School** is told only with the parents' agreement; a broken writing hand may need help in class.

**Records.** In England an injury claim can be brought until the child turns 21. **Inference:** keep accident reports at least that long. Georgian rules are an open question.

## 2. How GrassKickZ should handle it

One injury becomes **one case the club can follow**: opened by the coach at the pitch in under a minute, built from records the club already has, with the private parts private.

**Who sees what**

| Person | Sees |
|---|---|
| Coach of the squad | The report they filed, and the restriction in plain words ("Fitness only · no contact · review Thu"), on the player card, the session list and match selection. Never the diagnosis. |
| Medical lead (a club physio or doctor, else the head of academy) | Everything: clinical notes, appointments, clearance. |
| Parents and guardians | Everything about their child. They add what the doctor said and upload the letter. |
| The player (teen with their own account) | Their own return plan and the stage they're at. |
| Welfare officer | The case only if a welfare concern is raised. |
| Club admin | The injury register (how many, time lost, training or match) without diagnoses, for insurance and licensing. |

**The flow**
1. **Report, at the pitch, on a phone.** In My squads, open the session's attendance, choose the player, then "Report an injury".
   - **On top: the emergency card.**
     - The venue's emergency plan: the nearest hospital, the defibrillator, and a one-tap call to 112.
     - The child's emergency contacts in call order.
     - Whether the parents gave consent for emergency treatment.
   - **Pre-filled:** the player, the session, the time and place, the coach, and the other adults at the session.
   - **Taps, not typing:**
     - where it hurts (a body figure);
     - how (contact, fall, other);
     - the first aid given;
     - where the child went (home, emergency department, ambulance);
     - parents told: the app calls or messages them and records it.
   - **Saving creates three things:**
     - the injury report;
     - "left injured" on the attendance;
     - an automatic restriction, "No football until assessed".
2. **That evening, the parents** get it in Parent Hub: "Luka hurt his hand at training. Tell us what the doctor said."
   - They add the outcome: the diagnosis, the cast-until date, the follow-up appointment and the letter.
   - The diagnosis goes into a clinical note only the medical lead and guardians see.
3. **The restriction becomes specific**, set by the medical lead or from the doctor's letter: "Fitness only · no contact · no goalkeeping", with a review date.
   - The coach's player card shows the heart sign.
   - Sessions list the player as "fitness only".
   - Picking them for a match warns the coach.
4. **The weeks between:**
   - Appointments are records (scheduled, attended, cleared).
   - Parents get reminders before reviews.
   - Agent Dola tells the coach "Luka's review is Thursday", without the diagnosis.
5. **Return to play** steps through the four stages above, each step signed in the history.
   - Moving to full contact needs clearance: the doctor's letter, or the medical lead's sign-off.
   - When the restriction is cleared, the sign leaves the player card and the parents are told.
6. **Afterwards,** the report closes with the days lost, worked out from the dates.
   - It feeds the club's injury register using FIFA's fields: days lost, body area, training or match, how it happened, and whether it is a repeat.
   - An insurance claim can start from it.

## 3. Similar scenarios

| Scenario | What real clubs do | What the app does |
|---|---|---|
| **Suspected concussion** | "If in doubt, sit them out." No return that day, and red flags get urgent care. Every case is seen by a professional. School comes back before football. Full contact only after 14 symptom-free days; earliest match on day 21 (FA 2023, under-19s and adults alike). No fast track at 16 or under. | Choosing the head on the report applies the concussion protocol. Its stages are preset, the earliest dates are worked out and locked for under-16s, and parents get the school-first guidance. |
| **Heat illness** | Cool the child and give fluids; call an ambulance if they're no better within 30 minutes. Avoid 11:00–15:00; water, breaks, shade. | Heat warning on the session, with a suggestion to move it (the calendar move dialog tells families). Report it if it happens. |
| **Asthma attack** | Sit the child up; one puff of the reliever every 30–60 seconds, up to 10. Ambulance if no better. | With the parents' consent, the child's care plan shows on the emergency card for the staff at that session. |
| **Anaphylaxis** | Adrenaline auto-injector, call an ambulance, legs raised, a second dose after 5 minutes. | Same care plan and emergency card. |
| **Parent unreachable** | Call the emergency contacts in order. A named adult decides on emergency treatment (UEFA consent template). Treatment goes ahead if waiting is risky. | Call order and the medical-treatment consent on the emergency card. |
| **Welfare concern after an injury** | Stay calm, never promise secrecy, report to the welfare officer. Immediate risk goes to the police. | "This may be a welfare concern" on the report opens a confidential welfare concern. The coach can file it but not read it afterwards. |
| **Child not collected** | Two staff stay. Call the parents, then the emergency contacts. After an hour, ask the police or social services. No lone lifts. | "Not collected" on attendance shows the call order and tells the welfare officer. |
| **Stomach bug or flu** | Back 2 days after the sickness stops; flu once there's no fever (school guidance; using it for clubs is our inference). | The parent reports the absence as illness. No medical record; a short restriction ends by itself. |

## 4. What exists and what's missing

**Already in the club records (`/workspace-roles/work`)**
- Readiness: the emergency plan, with the nearest hospital, defibrillator, first-aid kit and responsible person.
- Guardians:
  - emergency contacts, in call order;
  - consent, including for medical treatment;
  - collection authorisation.
- Attendance, with injury as an absence reason.
- Medical: appointments (scheduled, attended, cleared) and clinical notes.
- Restrictions (active, cleared, ended), with a "can and cannot do" text and a review date.
- Welfare concerns.
- First-aid qualifications with an expiry date.

**Missing (backend work for later, not this sandbox)**
1. **An injury report record:** the NSPCC fields plus FIFA's. It anchors the case.
2. **Return-to-play stages** on a restriction, saying who may move each stage on. Protocols too, such as concussion with its locked earliest dates.
3. **Structured restrictions** (no contact, no goalkeeping, fitness only) instead of free text, so sessions and match selection can warn.
4. **A medical-lead scope** for clinical notes, plus parents as writers of their own child's outcome (the doctor's letter).
5. **Care plans** (asthma, allergies), shown with consent to the staff at a session.
6. **A first-aider at every session:** the session knows whether an adult with a valid first-aid qualification is there, and warns if not.
7. **Reads of medical records** kept in the history, and a retention period.
8. **Insurance claims** from the report.

**In the sandbox now:** the heart sign on player cards and the "Restricted" view on Players read the existing restriction records.

## 5. Open questions for the owner and the coaches

- Does FC Dinamo Tbilisi Academy have a youth-team physio or doctor? Licensed clubs need one (UEFA licensing, Article 41; GFF 2022, Article 42). Who should be the medical lead?
- Do Georgian clubs carry accident insurance for children, and who makes the claim?
- After an injury, do parents expect a phone call, or is a message enough? (Proposed: always call; the app records it.)
- From what age does a player see their own return plan? (Proposed: 13+, with their own account.)
- How long should the club keep injury reports under Georgian law?
